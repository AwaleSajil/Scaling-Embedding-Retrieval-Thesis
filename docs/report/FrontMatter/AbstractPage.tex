Dense vector embeddings are central to modern retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines and large-scale search systems, but their memory footprint becomes a critical bottleneck at scale. This thesis presents a controlled empirical comparison of post-hoc and training-time embedding compression methods, evaluated under identical training data and benchmark conditions across three encoder architectures: BGE-base-en-v1.5, RoBERTa-base, and MPNet-base. Seven compression methods are studied: INT8 scalar quantization, binary quantization, Product Quantization, TurboQuant, Straight-Through Estimator (STE) binarization, Annealed Tanh binarization, and Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL), including systematic stacked combinations that pair MRL dimensionality reduction with precision-reducing methods. \textbf{[TODO: 2--3 sentences of key results, e.g., main finding on training-time vs.\ post-hoc; which binarization strategy wins; MRL stacking outcome; cross-architecture consistency.]} Results are presented as Pareto curves on NanoBEIR, offering practitioners direct guidance on method selection under a given memory budget.
